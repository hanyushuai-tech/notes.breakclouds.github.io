Stage 1 — Vector spaces and the language of LA

This is where most physicists are tempted to skip ahead, because you already have intuition for vectors from mechanics. Resist that. The point here is to rebuild that intuition abstractly, which pays off enormously later.

Study the axiomatic definition of a vector space over a field. The payoff is realizing that the polynomials satisfying a boundary condition, the solutions to a homogeneous ODE, and the complex wave functions in a box are all vector spaces — your geometric intuition transfers to every one of them. The concepts of span, linear independence, and basis are the foundation for everything: in QM, the eigenstates of a Hamiltonian form a basis for Hilbert space in exactly the sense you define here.

Key goal: Be able to identify whether a given set is a vector space, find a basis, and determine dimension without coordinates.

Stage 2 — Linear maps and matrices

A matrix is not a linear map — it is the representation of a linear map in a chosen basis. This distinction is one of the most important things you can internalize. The rotation of a rigid body is a physical operation independent of how you set up coordinates; the rotation matrix R depends entirely on your choice of axes.

Work through: linear transformations as structure-preserving maps, the matrix of a transformation in a given basis, the change-of-basis formula A’ = P⁻¹AP, and the rank-nullity theorem. For physics, the most important cases are rotation matrices SO(3), the Lorentz transformation as a linear map on Minkowski space, and the Jones matrix formalism in optics.

Key goal: Given a linear transformation described in words or physics, construct its matrix; understand what happens when you change basis.

Stage 3 — Solving systems and decompositions

This is the computational workhorse. Gaussian elimination and LU decomposition should become second nature. More important conceptually is the rank-nullity theorem: the dimension of the null space (kernel) is the number of free parameters in the solution. In a statics problem, the null space of the force-balance matrix is the space of zero-force mechanisms.

Spend time here on the four fundamental subspaces (column space, null space, row space, left null space) — Gilbert Strang’s framing of this is exceptional. LU, QR, and Cholesky decompositions are worth understanding before you need them in numerical work.

Key goal: Characterize the solution set of Ax = b completely using rank and nullity; understand what each decomposition buys you numerically.

Stage 4 — Inner products and orthogonality

This stage elevates the vector space to a metric space — you can now measure lengths and angles. The inner product is where the geometry lives. Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization is the algorithm behind projection onto subspaces, which you will use constantly: least-squares fitting, projecting a state onto an eigenspace, decomposing a signal into normal modes.

The adjoint of a linear operator is defined here, which immediately sets up Stage 5. A critical point: over ℂ, the inner product is sesquilinear (conjugate-linear in one argument), which is why ⟨ψ|Aφ⟩ ≠ ⟨Aψ|φ⟩ in general, but does equal for a Hermitian operator.

Key goal: Be able to construct an orthonormal basis via Gram-Schmidt; understand the projection formula and its physical meaning (work = F·d̂ × |d|).

Stage 5 — Eigentheory and spectral theorems

This is the climax of finite-dimensional LA and the most physics-rich territory. Eigenvalues and eigenvectors arise whenever you look for “preferred directions” under a transformation — the principal axes of the inertia tensor, the normal modes of a coupled oscillator, the stationary states of a Hamiltonian.

The spectral theorem is the central result: a real symmetric (or complex Hermitian) matrix can be diagonalized in an orthonormal basis. This justifies everything you do in quantum mechanics — the eigenstates of a Hermitian observable form a complete orthonormal set. Make sure to also cover the Singular Value Decomposition (SVD), which works for any matrix and has deep geometric meaning: every linear map is a composition of a rotation, a rescaling, and another rotation.

Work through coupled oscillators as an extended example: you solve Mv̈ = −Kx, reduce to a generalized eigenvalue problem, and find the normal modes directly. It’s one of the most satisfying unifications in classical physics.

Key goal: Diagonalize a symmetric matrix; find principal axes; state and apply the spectral theorem; understand what SVD tells you about a map.

Stage 6 — Infinite dimensions and quantum operators

Everything from Stages 1–5 generalizes — with care. A Hilbert space is an infinite-dimensional inner product space that is complete (Cauchy sequences converge). Dirac notation is not magic; |ψ⟩ is a vector, ⟨φ| is a linear functional (an element of the dual space), and ⟨φ|ψ⟩ is the inner product. The spectral decomposition of a Hermitian operator is the generalization of diagonalization: A = ∫ λ d E(λ), where E(λ) is the spectral measure.

The Schrödinger equation iℏ ∂ₜ|ψ⟩ = H|ψ⟩ is a linear ODE in Hilbert space, and the time-evolution operator U(t) = e^{−iHt/ℏ} is unitary — a fact you can only appreciate after Stage 4. Sturm-Liouville theory connects back to LA here: the eigenfunctions of a Sturm-Liouville operator form a complete orthonormal set, which is why Fourier series and Legendre polynomials work. Fourier analysis itself is a change of basis from position to momentum eigenstates.

Key goal: Be fluent in Dirac notation; understand why observables must be Hermitian; connect the functional-analytic machinery to specific QM problems.

Running in parallel throughout

Computational practice is non-negotiable. Every abstract result should be implemented — eigendecompose a 3×3 inertia tensor, run SVD on a noisy dataset, solve a coupled ODE via matrix exponentiation. NumPy/SciPy in Python or Julia are the natural environments.

For texts, the canonical path is Axler’s Linear Algebra Done Right for the abstract foundations (it delays matrices deliberately, which builds real understanding), Strang’s Introduction to Linear Algebra for geometric and applied intuition, and Shankar’s Principles of Quantum Mechanics as the physics bridge (its first chapter is an exceptional standalone LA course). Arfken’s Mathematical Methods covers the parts most relevant to graduate-level physics.

The biggest pitfall to avoid is confusing a vector with its coordinate representation. When you write a ket |ψ⟩ in two different bases, the vector is the same physical object — only the column of numbers changes. Keeping this separation sharp will save you enormous confusion in tensor analysis and QFT later.

第一阶段 — 向量空间与线性代数的语言

这一阶段是许多物理学家最容易跳过的地方，因为你已经从力学中对向量有了直观感受。请抵制这种冲动。这里的要点是抽象地重建那种直觉——这在后续会带来巨大的回报。

学习域上的向量空间的公理化定义。这样做的回报在于，你会意识到：满足边界条件的多项式、齐次常微分方程的解、以及盒中的复波函数——它们都是向量空间。你的几何直觉可以直接迁移到每一个对象上。张成、线性无关和基的概念是所有后续内容的基础：在量子力学中，哈密顿量的本征态恰好是在这里定义的基，构成了希尔伯特空间的一组基。

关键目标： 能够判断给定的集合是否为向量空间，找到一组基，并且在不依赖坐标的情况下确定维数。

第二阶段 — 线性映射与矩阵

矩阵并不是线性映射——它是线性映射在选定基下的表示。这个区别是你能够内化的最重要的事情之一。刚体的旋转是一种与坐标选择无关的物理操作；而旋转矩阵 R 则完全取决于你对坐标轴的选择。

学习内容：作为保结构映射的线性变换、变换在给定基下的矩阵、基变换公式 A’ = P⁻¹AP，以及秩-零化度定理。对物理学而言，最重要的例子包括 SO(3) 旋转矩阵、作为闵可夫斯基空间上线性映射的洛伦兹变换，以及光学中的琼斯矩阵形式。

关键目标： 给定用文字或物理语言描述的线性变换，构造其矩阵；理解当基改变时矩阵发生了什么变化。

第三阶段 — 求解系统与矩阵分解

这是计算层面的主力工具。高斯消元法和 LU 分解应当成为你的第二天性。更重要的是概念上的秩-零化度定理：零空间（核）的维数就是解中自由参数的个数。在静力学问题中，力平衡矩阵的零空间就是零机构运动的空间。

花时间掌握四个基本子空间（列空间、零空间、行空间、左零空间）——Gilbert Strang 对此的阐述非常出色。LU、QR 和 Cholesky 分解值得你在数值计算中需要它们之前就先行理解。

关键目标： 利用秩和零化度完全刻画 Ax = b 的解集；理解每种分解在数值上带来的好处。

第四阶段 — 内积与正交性

这一阶段将向量空间提升为度量空间——现在你可以测量长度和角度了。内积是几何的所在。Gram-Schmidt 正交化是子空间投影背后的算法，你将不断用到它：最小二乘拟合、将态投影到本征空间、将信号分解为简正模式。

伴随算子的定义也在此给出，它直接为第五阶段奠定了基础。一个关键点：在复数域 ℂ 上，内积是半双线性的（对其中一个参数是共轭线性的），这就是为什么一般来说 ⟨ψ|Aφ⟩ ≠ ⟨Aψ|φ⟩，但对于厄米算子而言二者相等。

关键目标： 能够通过 Gram-Schmidt 方法构造标准正交基；理解投影公式及其物理意义（功 = F·d̂ × |d|）。

第五阶段 — 本征理论与谱定理

这是有限维线性代数的顶点，也是物理学内涵最丰富的领域。特征值和特征向量出现在你寻找变换下的“优选方向”时——惯性张量的主轴、耦合振子的简正模式、哈密顿量的定态。

谱定理是核心结果：实对称（或复厄米）矩阵可以在标准正交基下对角化。这为你在量子力学中做的一切提供了理论基础——厄米可观测量的一组本征态构成一个完备的标准正交集。确保也要掌握奇异值分解（SVD），它适用于任何矩阵并具有深刻的几何含义：每个线性映射都可以分解为一个旋转、一个缩放和另一个旋转的复合。

通过一个扩展例子学习耦合振子：求解 Mv̈ = −Kx，将其化为广义特征值问题，并直接找到简正模式。这是经典物理学中最令人满意的统一之一。

关键目标： 对称矩阵的对角化；找到主轴；叙述并应用谱定理；理解 SVD 告诉我们关于一个映射的哪些信息。

第六阶段 — 无限维与量子力学中的算子

第一至第五阶段的所有内容都可以推广到无限维——但需要小心处理。希尔伯特空间是一个完备（柯西序列收敛）的无限维内积空间。狄拉克记号并非魔法；|ψ⟩ 是一个向量，⟨φ| 是一个线性泛函（属于对偶空间），而 ⟨φ|ψ⟩ 是内积。厄米算子的谱分解是对角化概念的推广：A = ∫ λ d E(λ)，其中 E(λ) 是谱测度。

薛定谔方程 iℏ ∂ₜ|ψ⟩ = H|ψ⟩ 是希尔伯特空间上的线性常微分方程，时间演化算子 U(t) = e^{−iHt/ℏ} 是幺正的——这个事实只有在第四阶段之后你才能真正理解。Sturm-Liouville 理论在这里与线性代数产生联系：一个 Sturm-Liouville 算子的本征函数构成一组完备的标准正交系，这就是傅里叶级数和勒让德多项式有效的原因。傅里叶分析本身是从位置本征态到动量本征态的基变换。

关键目标： 熟练掌握狄拉克记号；理解可观测量为什么必须是厄米的；将泛函分析的机制与具体的量子力学问题联系起来。

贯穿始终、并行进行的内容

计算练习是不可妥协的。每一个抽象结果都应该用代码实现——对 3×3 的惯性张量进行特征分解，对带噪声的数据集运行 SVD，通过矩阵指数求解耦合常微分方程组。Python 中的 NumPy/SciPy 或 Julia 是自然的实践环境。

关于教材，经典路径是：Axler 的《Linear Algebra Done Right》用于抽象的 foundations（它有意延迟引入矩阵，这能建立真正的理解），Strang 的《Introduction to Linear Algebra》用于几何与应用的直觉，以及 Shankar 的《Principles of Quantum Mechanics》作为与物理的桥梁（它的第一章本身就是一门出色的线性代数课程）。Arfken 的《Mathematical Methods》涵盖了与研究生物理最相关的部分。

需要避免的最大陷阱是混淆向量与其坐标表示。当你把同一个态 |ψ⟩ 写在两个不同的基下时，这个向量是同一个物理对象——改变的只是那一列数字。保持这一区分的清晰，将在你后续学习张量分析和量子场论时省去巨大的困惑。
